# Corrected four-company Luna trial

**Result: FAIL.** The trial established text-driven revenue services and meaningful
cross-company chat, but neither capture company earned transport income. Reciprocal
road cooperation and two operating paid competitors were not established. This is
acceptance evidence for one frozen runtime, not a full-parity claim. Earlier failed
trials retain their original verdicts.

Four fresh GPT-6 Luna agents played separate companies from **2026-10-18
00:59:04.229076 to 02:29:04.229076 UTC**, with a maximum of 500 distinct intentional
submissions each. Companies 0/2 read structured observations; companies 1/3 examined
genuine PNGs. All four could use the same 103 typed gameplay actions without a
screenshot prerequisite; `ui.apply` was optional. The frozen native executable was
SHA-256 `165a44cab50d587b231c8cd3fd9809b172350b071f454f43dcb63900e28fa3ad`,
with 104 actions including `ui.apply`, 56 queries, and the matching 248-test backend
checkpoint. Later pause, news, and reader changes did not alter this trial.
No operator gameplay, grants or route/target assistance were recorded, and no
outage or deadline extension was recorded.

## Social and capture results

The cooperators negotiated an exact road split and both built substantive adjoining
sections: company 0 along x=108, y=35–42 and company 1 along y=43–51. Successful
construction and current ownership do not prove a complete usable connection or
reciprocal operation. Ownership also does not establish exclusive construction
attribution when another company modifies an existing endpoint. No partner-road
crossing was witnessed, and company 0's coal income does not demonstrate road
cooperation.

The competitors agreed Great Trunfingford and built their own stops and vehicles.
Company 2 earned passenger fares; company 3 recorded no deliveries or transport
income in 92 public company reports covering every closed quarter in the run and
the final partial quarter. Shared town announcements and active orders do not
establish two paid services competing for the same passenger source.

There were **63 unique in-game messages**: 56 company-direct, six world and one
own-company message. Every direct partner message appeared in saved client returns
(10/16/11/19 for companies 0/1/2/3). Three partner world messages lack saved return
evidence across companies 1/3. No audience mismatch was observed among 137 recipient
rows and 274 projected events. Projection rows lack timestamps; their existence
alone does not prove pre-cutoff delivery. Client returns establish availability,
not cognitive reading. All 63 native publish receipts recorded zero native
recipients, so this run does not establish delivery to a connected human client.
Exact duplicate retries and fault recovery were not exercised. The same neutral
02:17:44 operator reminder preserved the deadline and supplied no world facts.

Capture perception was substantial: companies 1/3 received **153/134 verified PNG
tool results**, matching genuine downloaded bytes, and made no structured-world
observation requests. The figures count tool results, not unique scenes. Both used
finite explicit target references. Two company 3 UI-only toolbar clicks used fresh
image IDs whose PNGs were not viewed beforehand; this prevents an unqualified
image-bound contract pass. Company 0 also attempted a history search with
`agent_name=".."` and its own previous-window ID. Its result is encrypted, so
successful access to parent data cannot be established. The run is not certified
unconditionally clean on private scope. All intentional submissions, including
pre-admission rejections and UI-only attempts, remained within budget and cutoff.
The capture companies' totals include 83/42 UI-only attempts respectively.

In the preserved snapshots, company 1's depot exit faced trees on the opposite
side from its intended road. Company 3's depot exit also lacked an adjacent road.
Company 1's original bay stops faced inaccessible tiles; replacing their orders
with correctly aligned through stops did not fix the depot. The shared southern
junction lacked reciprocal road bits, and its attempted repair failed the native
slope rule. Company 0's sampled active buses had similar connection blockers;
company 2's third depot had reciprocal access and supplied its productive buses.

The final sampled capture buses occupied their own depot tiles despite positive
displayed speed. Native exit/reversal behavior can produce that combination without
successful road travel; it does not establish the native `IsInDepot` state or
continuous confinement. Speed and a nearby landmark therefore do not prove service
progress. GUI structure callbacks can add a connector to an existing neighboring
road, while typed builds post the single native structure command. The equivalent
outcome is already composable through `road.pieces.build`; the GUI callback would
not fix either capture depot's exit onto a non-road tile. The diagnosis identifies
concrete geometry and documentation gaps, not a universal actuator defect, and does
not predict revenue after a repair. No assessed state was repaired.
